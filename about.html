Who I Am

I'm Kelvin Liu, a third-year Computer Science student in York University's Lassonde co-op program, expected to graduate in April 2029. I build interactive web applications with React and TypeScript, and I enjoy turning complex ideas, like how an algorithm changes a data structure, into interfaces people can see and use. My path into software started with building my own gaming PC, and four years of part-time work in a busy kitchen taught me to stay calm, communicate clearly and deliver under pressure. I'm seeking a Winter 2028 co-op where I can contribute to a real engineering team while growing as a developer.

Career Goals

In the short term, I want to gain hands-on experience as a software developer through co-op, ideally on a team building web applications, where I can learn professional practices like code review, testing and deployment. My Journey

From gamer to PC builder to developer: how I got here, one step at a time.

  1. Childhood

    The spark: Counter-Strike 1.6

    Playing Counter-Strike 1.6 got me hooked on games, and on wondering how they actually worked.

  2. Feb 2022 – Present

    Kitchen Staff, Sansotei Ramen

    I took my first job to save for a gaming PC. Since then I've been part of a six-person team across multiple stations, serving 100+ covers on busy weekend nights. Four years of reliable part-time work alongside full-time school taught me teamwork, time management and accountability.

  3. High school

    Building my first PC

    I planned and built my own desktop, balancing looks, performance and budget. Troubleshooting it, and later building PCs for friends, made me curious about the software running on them.

  4. May – Jun 2023

    My first real program

    I built a Three Card Poker game in Java with a full Swing GUI. It was the first time I designed a program around clean object-oriented structure instead of just making it work.

  5. Sep 2024

    Computer Science at York University

    Started a BSc Honours in Computer Science (Co-op) at the Lassonde School of Engineering, with coursework in data structures, advanced OOP, databases, computer organization and probability.

  6. Oct 2025

    Shipping a game in 7 days

    Entered a Halloween game jam on itch.io with three friends, learned Godot from scratch in a day, and shipped Grease Trap, a drive-thru horror game that placed in the top 30% of 1,000+ submissions. It was my first time building and shipping software as a team.

  7. May 2026 – Present

    Building for the web

    Shipped VisualOS, an algorithm visualizer in React and TypeScript, and started Pokemon Team Analyzer, my first full-stack app with a Node/Express backend and an LLM integration.

  8. Next

    My first software co-op

    I'm looking for a Winter 2028 co-op term to bring this curiosity to a real engineering team. Let's talk.

Beyond the Code

The interests behind my projects, and behind the look of this site.

Transformers

Transformers has been part of my life since childhood. I started with G1, then moved on to the live-action movies. When I turned 18 I figured I had grown out of it, but it never really left. Once I had my own income, I bought the figure I had wanted as a kid, and it reminded me how much I still enjoy the franchise. I live by the saying:

We don't stop playing because we grow old; we grow old because we stop playing.

It's also why this site has the crashed Ark in the background, Optimus Prime on the home page and an Autobot logo in the nav.

PC building

I grew up on competitive games like Overwatch, Valorant and Counter-Strike, but for years I only had an aging gaming laptop that struggled to keep up. That pushed me to get my first job and save for a desktop. I put real thought into balancing how it looked against how well it ran the games I played, within my budget. Booting it up for the first time was incredibly satisfying.

Building it taught me to troubleshoot, and I went on to build PCs for friends. Along the way I became curious about how the software side works, which is what led me to study computer science.

Pokemon

I've played the games for as long as I can remember. Chasing the perfect six-member team is what led to Pokemon Team Analyzer.

Game dev

After years of playing games, I wanted to make one. My team's drive-thru horror game Grease Trap placed in the top 30% of a 1,000+ entry game jam.
